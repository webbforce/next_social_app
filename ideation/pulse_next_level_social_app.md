# Pulse — Next-Level Social Application Concept

## Objective

Build a breakout social product designed around two goals:

1. **Grow the audience extremely quickly**
2. **Become strategically attractive for acquisition within ~12 months**

The product should not be another public feed. Instead, it should create a new social behavior that is easy to explain, naturally viral, and valuable to a larger platform.

---

## Core Concept

**Working name: Pulse**

A real-time social coordination network that turns:

> “What are my people doing right now?”

into actual interaction — and then automatically turns those interactions into shareable content.

Think:

**WhatsApp group chat × BeReal × Partiful × TikTok-style distribution × an AI social coordinator**

The key shift is:

> **Social media → social action**

Instead of opening the app and scrolling an infinite feed, users see what friends are doing, what they want to do, and who is available.

Example:

> **8 friends are around**
>
> Emma — wants coffee ☕  
> Thomas — gym in ~30 min 💪  
> Lisa + 3 — drinks later 🍸  
> 4 friends — “nothing planned” 👀
>
> **✨ AI: You, Emma and Thomas are all free 16:00–18:00. Want me to make something happen?**

One tap could create:

> “Coffee around 16:30?”

Pulse can then suggest a convenient place, create a temporary group, and invite everyone.

Afterward, shared photos and videos become an automatically generated collaborative mini-story.

---

## Product Structure

The product should revolve around a small number of core screens.

| Screen | Function |
|---|---|
| **Now** | What friends are doing or want to do |
| **Circles** | Small private friend groups |
| **Do** | Create a spontaneous activity, challenge, or meetup |
| **Moments** | Shared photos and video from things you did |
| **Replay** | AI-created weekly/monthly memory of the group |

The home screen should be deliberately **finite**.

A user should be able to understand what is happening among their friends within roughly 20 seconds.

Avoid making follower counts the center of the experience.

The fundamental object is not a **post**.

It is a **moment involving multiple people**.

---

# The Killer Mechanic

The app needs one behavior people can explain in one sentence.

Examples from previous social products:

- Facebook: “Find everyone you know.”
- Instagram: “Post beautiful photos.”
- Snapchat: “Photos that disappear.”
- TikTok: “Endless personalized videos.”
- BeReal: “Everyone posts when the notification goes off.”

For Pulse:

> **“See which of your friends are up for doing something right now.”**

Then add the secondary mechanic:

> **“Every time you do something together, the app creates the story for you.”**

This gives the product both:

- **Utility**, which supports retention
- **Content**, which supports distribution

---

# AI Should Be Invisible

AI should not be the product category.

It should operate behind the scenes as a **social catalyst**.

Examples:

> “Five people in your group haven’t seen each other in 19 days.”

> “Four friends appear free Friday night.”

> “Everyone saved Italian restaurants recently.”

> “Sarah is visiting Amsterdam this weekend.”

Then:

> **“Want me to organize something?”**

AI could suggest:

- Who to invite
- When to meet
- Where to go
- What activity fits the group
- What challenge to launch

Afterward, AI creates:

- A recap video
- A photo collage
- A meme
- A highlight reel
- A group “episode”

Eventually the interface could support prompts such as:

> “Make this weekend more interesting.”

The AI could then create several plans involving the user’s actual friends.

---

# The Viral Loop

The most important metric is not raw downloads.

The product needs to make **one user naturally expose several other people to the app**.

A typical loop could be:

**Alice creates something → invites 5 friends → friends participate without installing → photos accumulate → Pulse creates an attractive recap → participants share it externally → their friends discover Pulse → new groups form**

## Important: Do Not Require Installation Immediately

A shared WhatsApp or iMessage link could open a lightweight web experience:

> “Tom wants to get drinks tonight.  
> 7 people are already in.”

Button:

> **I’m in**

The recipient should be able to participate before installing the app.

Only later:

> “Get Pulse to see who’s around next time.”

This reduces friction in the invitation funnel.

---

# Use Existing Social Platforms as Distribution

TikTok’s distribution advantage was not limited to content consumed inside TikTok.

TikTok videos spread through:

- Instagram
- Twitter/X
- WhatsApp
- Reddit
- Group chats
- Other platforms

Pulse should deliberately create content people want to distribute externally.

Example recap:

## FRIDAY REPLAY

- 6 people
- 3 locations
- 127 photos
- 2:41 AM last survivor
- 😂 Quote of the night

Then:

A polished AI-generated 10–20 second montage.

At the end:

> **made with Pulse**

Existing social networks become a distribution channel for the product.

---

# Missions

“Missions” could become the entertainment layer of the app.

Examples:

- “Everyone photograph what you’re looking at. NOW.”
- “€20 night challenge.”
- “Worst outfit wins.”
- “First person to get outside gets 2x.”
- “Find the weirdest thing within 500m.”
- “Everyone recreate a photo from 5 years ago.”

A friend starts one.

Others join.

Content accumulates.

AI packages the results.

Groups can challenge other groups.

This creates another viral unit:

> **Circle → challenge → another circle**

That could be especially powerful on university campuses.

---

# Initial Market

Do not launch globally.

Start with a dense environment where social connections overlap heavily.

A strong starting market is:

## 18–24-year-old university students

Possible launch strategy:

- Pick one university
- Pick one city
- Achieve unusually high penetration
- Then replicate the same playbook elsewhere

For example:

> 14,000 users at one university  
> 72% of the target population  
> 48% DAU/MAU

That is potentially more strategically interesting than:

> 100,000 users scattered across Europe

Dense adoption helps demonstrate a genuine network effect.

Possible expansion path:

**Amsterdam → Rotterdam → Utrecht → London → Berlin → Paris**

Each city becomes a new ignition point.

---

# Acquisition Strategy

If the objective is a sale within about 12 months, the company should be built differently from a startup planning for a decade of independence.

Do not prioritize ad revenue early.

Instead, build something a strategic buyer looks at and thinks:

> **“We cannot let someone else own this behavior or social graph.”**

The pitch should not ultimately be:

> “We built another social app.”

It should become:

> **“We discovered a new consumer behavior, and it is growing rapidly and organically.”**

Potential strategic buyer categories could include companies focused on:

- Messaging
- Consumer social
- Camera and creation tools
- Events
- Local discovery
- AI assistants
- Friend discovery
- Gen Z social behavior

---

# Metrics to Obsess Over

Downloads alone are a vanity metric.

The internal acquisition dashboard should focus on:

| Metric | Aspirational Direction |
|---|---:|
| Invite recipients per new user | **3+** |
| % joining with existing friends | **Very high** |
| D1 retention | **>60% aspiration** |
| D7 retention | **>35–40% aspiration** |
| D30 retention | **>20–25% aspiration** |
| DAU / MAU | **>40% aspiration** |
| Weekly activities created/user | **Rising** |
| % activities involving 3+ people | **Rising** |
| External shares/user/week | **>1** |
| Organic installs | **70%+** |
| CAC | **Approaching zero** |

These are not universal acquisition requirements. They are aggressive internal targets intended to demonstrate:

> **Retention + graph density + organic distribution**

at the same time.

# 12-Month Execution Strategy

## Months 0–2 — Find the Atomic Behavior

Build almost nothing except:

- Now
- Friends
- Invite
- Activity creation
- Recap

Test with perhaps 100–500 people.

Change the product rapidly until groups voluntarily return.

The goal is to identify the single behavior people repeatedly want.

---

## Months 2–4 — Engineer Virality

Concentrate on one university or dense community.

Measure every invite path.

Build:

- Web participation
- External recap sharing
- Missions
- Group-to-group challenges

The priority is not marketing volume.

It is maximizing the viral coefficient of the product.

---

## Months 4–6 — Prove Replication

Launch in several new dense communities.

The key question becomes:

> Can the same playbook repeatedly take a community from zero to meaningful density?

A repeatable launch system is more valuable than a one-off viral spike.

---

## Months 6–9 — Expand Aggressively

Possible growth channels:

- Student ambassadors
- Campus organizations
- Creator seeding
- Nightlife integrations
- Events
- Group challenges
- Festivals
- Travel groups

Avoid generic paid acquisition unless it amplifies an already-working organic loop.

---

## Months 8–12 — Prepare Strategic Exit

Do not wait until month 12 to begin strategic conversations.

By this stage, potential buyers should clearly understand:

- What unique behavior the product owns
- Why users return
- How fast communities grow
- Whether the network effect is defensible
- Whether the behavior fits their ecosystem
- Whether the product is spreading organically

The ideal acquisition story is:

> **“This team has discovered a new form of social behavior and already proved that it replicates across communities.”**

---

# What Not to Build

Avoid distracting features during year one.

Do not prioritize:

- Generic TikTok feed
- Long-form profiles
- Facebook Groups clone
- Marketplace
- Advertising system
- Creator monetization infrastructure
- Complex desktop experience
- Cryptocurrency or token systems
- AI influencers
- Anonymous stranger messaging
- Large collections of reactions and cosmetic features

Every feature should strengthen one of three things:

1. **Retention**
2. **Virality**
3. **Strategic value**

---

# The Long-Term Experience

The ideal experience should eventually feel less like an app people browse and more like a **multiplayer layer over real life**.

Example:

> **Pulse**
>
> 11 friends are available tonight.
>
> 4 want drinks.  
> 3 want food.  
> 2 don’t care.  
> Sarah and Max haven’t met but share 6 friends.
>
> **I found a place everyone likes with a table at 20:00.**
>
> **Make it happen →**

One button.

Several hours later:

> **Your Friday episode is ready.**

This is the conceptual progression:

> **Instagram asks:** “What should I post?”  
> **TikTok asks:** “What should I watch?”  
> **Pulse asks:** **“What should we do?”**

That is the product direction I would pursue if the explicit objective is to create a fast-growing social application with the possibility of a strategic acquisition within roughly one year.
